**Figure 2.** Distribution of the frame score (−2 = the AI is the menace, +2 = the AI is the hero) in (a) all core films, (b) films with at least 100 TMDB votes and (c) high-confidence codings only. BC is Pfister et al.'s bimodality coefficient with a bootstrap 95% CI; values above 0.555 indicate two modes. In every population the distribution is single-peaked at 0, which does not support the two-frame claim.

`fig02_frame_score_distribution.png` / `fig02_frame_score_distribution.pdf`

**Figure 3.** How the frame of AI films has changed by decade (all 2,015 core films). (a) Composition of each decade; the number above each bar is the decade's film count. (b) Share of threat, benefit and mixed framings with Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Frame × decade: χ² = 60.1, df = 21, p < 0.001, Cramér's V = 0.10. Logistic trend of threat versus benefit: odds ratio per decade 0.90 (95% CI 0.84–0.96, p = 0.002).

`fig03_frame_by_decade.png` / `fig03_frame_by_decade.pdf`

**Figure 4.** The physical form of the AI. (a) Dominant embodiment by decade among core films with a determinable form (n above each bar). (b) Share of films per year whose AI is disembodied or a virtual human (years with fewer than 5 films omitted), with a 5-year centred rolling share. The best single changepoint in the share is 2023 (bootstrap 95% CI 2016–2023; share 17% before versus 52% after, n = 1,870), not the 1960s.

**Figure 5.** Share of films in which the AI turns on its creators (the Frankenstein complex), by decade, among films with a determinable answer, with Wilson 95% CIs; decades with fewer than 15 such films are omitted. Logistic trend, odds ratio per decade: All core films 0.93 (95% CI 0.87–0.99, p = 0.02); >= 100 TMDB votes 0.96 (95% CI 0.82–1.13, p = 0.62); High-confidence codings 1.04 (95% CI 0.94–1.16, p = 0.42).

**Figure 6.** Audience reception by coding. (a) Mean IMDb rating with 95% CI for the 412 core films with at least 1,000 IMDb votes; group sizes in parentheses, the vertical line is the grand mean (5.82). (b) Median box-office return (revenue ÷ budget) by frame for the 151 films with a reported budget of at least US$1 million and revenue, with a bootstrap 95% CI. Differences in (a) shrink substantially once year, popularity and language are controlled (see Table 5), so they are descriptive rather than causal.

`fig06_reception.png` / `fig06_reception.pdf`

**Figure 7.** Gender presentation of lead and supporting AI characters by decade (characters coded 'unknown' or 'mixed' excluded), with Wilson 95% CIs. (a) All films. (b) Films with at least 100 TMDB votes, where 'unknown' is rare. Logistic trends, odds ratio per decade: in (a) male 0.82 (p < 0.001), female 1.22 (p < 0.001); in (b) male 0.98 (p = 0.76), female 1.23 (p = 0.007). The steep male decline in (a) is driven by obscure recent films; among well-known films the male share is flat and the female share rises.

`fig07_character_gender_by_decade.png` / `fig07_character_gender_by_decade.pdf`

**Figure 8.** Stance of the 3,263 AI characters. (a) Stance by kind of body (character counts in parentheses). (b) Share of characters coded antagonist by gender presentation within each kind, with Wilson 95% CIs; cells with fewer than 15 characters omitted. Body outranks gender: disembodied and ungendered AIs are the antagonists, while female-presented AIs are no more hostile than male ones within the same kind.

`fig08_character_stance.png` / `fig08_character_stance.pdf`

**Figure 9.** Stance of lead and supporting AI characters by gender presentation (n = 2,159; characters coded 'unknown' or 'mixed' excluded). (a) Composition of each gender; character counts in parentheses. (b) The same shares with Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Gender × stance: χ² = 212.9, df = 6, p < 0.001, Cramér's V = 0.22. Male-presented AIs are most often allies, female-presented AIs are most often coded ambiguous, and ungendered AIs are the antagonists; female AIs are no more likely than male ones to be antagonists.

`fig09_character_gender_by_stance.png` / `fig09_character_gender_by_stance.pdf`

**Figure 10.** Share of lead and supporting AI characters coded antagonist, by gender presentation and decade, with Wilson 95% CIs; cells with fewer than 15 characters omitted. (a) All films. (b) Films with at least 100 TMDB votes. Logistic trend in the odds of being an antagonist, per decade: female AIs 0.93 (95% CI 0.82–1.04, p = 0.21) in (a) and 0.96 (95% CI 0.73–1.26, p = 0.75) in (b); male AIs 0.93 (95% CI 0.85–1.01, p = 0.08) in (a) and 0.95 (95% CI 0.80–1.12, p = 0.53) in (b). A female × decade interaction term (female versus male trend) gives an odds ratio of 1.00 (95% CI 0.86–1.15, p = 0.99) in (a) and 1.01 (95% CI 0.73–1.39, p = 0.95) in (b). There is no evidence that female AI characters have become more villainous over time; the two trends are indistinguishable.

`fig10_antagonist_by_gender_over_time.png` / `fig10_antagonist_by_gender_over_time.pdf`

**Figure 11.** The 111 core films with at least 50,000 IMDb votes placed on the frame scale by release year. Marker area grows with the log of the vote count; colour is the coded frame; points are spread vertically within a score to reduce overlap. 34 of the 40 most-voted films are labelled (the rest would overlap). The films most people have seen cluster around the middle of the scale rather than at its poles.
